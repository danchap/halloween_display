# Building the spider

Written 2026-10-13 for the 2026-10-31 display. Prices and delivery dates
are what Amazon.fr and the stores showed on 2026-10-13 for an order from
Paris; a logged-in Prime account may show earlier dates. Links are listed
once in the parts tables. The page at
https://danielsknowledge.com/halloween/ shows the same parts in 3D
(Construction section) and prints the cut list, the elbow pair and twist
at every joint and the part counts for whatever pose is chosen (live
since the evening of 2026-10-13). The quantities below are the page's for
the pose it starts from, with spares: L = 0.8 m, the whole spider
pitched up 45° (climbing up and out toward the street), the legs fanned
out in plan and the feet at 3.0, 2.6, 2.3 and 2.0 m from the front pair
to the back, none within a child's reach; the pose must be settled
before the hub is built (see Schedule).

## The build in one paragraph

Eight legs of PVC evacuation pipe Ø 40 mm in black foam sleeves, bent at
the knee by a fixed PVC elbow or a pair of them, meet a spine of Ø 40
pipe with four cross fittings inside the body. Each foot stands on a
vertical post of Ø 50 pipe, and the four posts of a side plug into a Ø 50
pipe lying along the base of that wall (the ski), so nothing stands in
the alley. Cords from each post top to sandbags on the ground brace the
posts along the walls; sandbags on the skis stop them rolling. The body
is black stretch fabric over a taped bin bag, shaped by pool-noodle
hoops zip-tied to the spine. Every joint that sets an angle is set by
turning the fitting in its socket and locked with two screws, so it
comes apart with a screwdriver; the knee elbows and the hub (spine,
crosses, stubs and the hip elbow pairs, set at their angles in Paris)
are glued because they carry the most load, and each upper leg is
screwed into its hip pair. The walls step 2 to 7 cm at the feet along
the alley, so the page's "Posts plumb" setting (on by default) puts each
side's feet on one straight line at the innermost wall and every post
stands vertical; the gap between the ski's face and a wall that steps
out is packed with offcuts, and where a wall turns away at the alley
mouth the ski's end stands further off (0.17 m on the right in the
default pose), so that overhang is trimmed or packed with a block; the
page gives the gap. In this plan only the hub (about 0.6 m
across with its pairs on), the fittings and the body kit leave Paris;
the pipe is bought on the way to the island or delivered there.

## What the research settled

- No adjustable or swivelling elbow exists in Ø 40 PVC (Nicoll,
  Interplast, Wirquin, Fitt, First Plast, Girpi, RIBON were checked).
  What exists is fixed elbows of 20°, 30°, 45°, 67.5° and 87.5°, a
  cuttable RIBON elbow (5/10/15° or 15/25/35°) and the Wirquin Magicoude
  bellows, which bends to any angle but has no stiffness. Any angle from
  0° to 155° comes from two fixed elbows in series, twisted against each
  other; the page computes which two and the twist for every joint, and
  the pairs for the brief defaults are tabled under Cut list below.
- Posts of Ø 40 pipe are too weak where children can reach: a child
  hanging on a 1.6 m foot or a 200 N push at mid height buckles or bends
  a 2.9 m post. Ø 50 × 3 mm posts have a safety factor of 2.5 in a
  60 km/h wind and 1.5 on a 200 N push along the wall with the top
  stayed. Unbraced, a post folds at its ski tee under a 15 N push, so
  the cord stays are not optional, and a post is never left standing
  without them. The stays hold pushes along the wall only; a push toward
  the alley centre is held by the leg acting as a strut (about 100 N per
  post top with glued knees) and by the ballast friction of the ski,
  unless the post pairs are tied to hooks in the walls.
- The knee and hub joints carry 25 to 40 N m from the spider's own
  weight; a screwed push-fit socket holds 20 to 30 N m. Those joints are
  glued. The one screwed socket left at each hip (pair to upper leg)
  sees 15 to 25 N m, a safety factor of about 1, and every screwed
  socket has about 0.9° of play, so expect the hub to settle 10 to 25 cm
  below the page's height. Before the knees are glued on the island,
  hang 5 kg at 1 m from one screwed and one glued elbow in Paris
  overnight and look for creep. The other joints carry much less and
  are screwed.
- Paris to the island by parcel: Chronopost takes a box up to 150 cm
  long (length plus twice width plus twice height under 300 cm, 30 kg)
  with free collection at the flat, next working day, about 29 € a box;
  Colissimo stops at 100 cm; UPS through a packer (Mail Boxes Etc.) goes
  to 274 cm with surcharges above 100 cm. Nobody parcels a 4 m ski.
- Amazon.fr delivers to Les Portes-en-Ré at the normal Prime speed; the
  nearest Amazon locker is at the Leclerc in Saint-Martin-de-Ré. Leroy
  Merlin Puilboreau (on the road in from the A10, 51 min from the house,
  open Sunday 9 to 19), Castorama Aytré and Brico Dépôt Andilly all offer
  free collection two hours after an online order; Bricoloisirs in
  Ars-en-Ré (13 min) and Bricomarché in La Flotte (30 min) cover top-ups.

## Cut list and joint angles

The Numbers panel on the page lists, for the pose on screen, every pipe
length between fittings (allowing 27 mm of insertion at each end and the
fitting's own offset), the elbow or elbow pair and twist for each hip,
knee and foot, the post heights, the ski and spine pieces and the stay
lengths. Dry-fit each elbow pair, measure it, and check a cut before
cutting the rest: the socket depths of the DOJA and FLUXI-TECH fittings
were not published and are taken as Nicoll's 27 mm.

How a pair makes an angle: two elbows a and b plugged together give any
bend between a − b and a + b. Twisting the second elbow about the
first's outlet by ψ gives the bend θ with cos θ = cos a · cos b −
sin a · sin b · cos ψ; the page prints ψ. The pair is then turned as a
whole in the socket that holds it, to point the leg: at the hip this
sets how far up the leg rises, at the foot the post's own rotation in
the ski tee sets the leg's direction along the alley. Mark the position
with a pen, drill 3 mm through socket and spigot, and drive two 4.2 × 16
screws per socket at 90° apart.

## Assembly, in order

1. Paris, before leaving: build the hub from one 2 m Ø 40 length bought
   with the 20° elbows. Cut the spine pieces and the stubs, push the
   four crosses on with the spine pieces between them, glue them. Set
   each hip pair to its twist from the table (stub into the 45's socket,
   the 45's spigot into the 20's socket), glue the two elbows together,
   glue the pair onto its stub and glue a Ø 40 coupler onto the pair's
   spigot; the coupler's free socket takes the upper leg on the island.
   An adult glues, at an open window; the
   cement takes a day before it carries load. Build the body kit: make
   nothing yet, pack fabric, bags, noodles, ties, eyes, tape. Sort the
   fittings per leg into eight labelled bags with each leg's cut list,
   a Ø 40 coupler for the knee in each.
2. Island, day 1: collect the pipe. Cut the leg pieces to the list (the
   kids measure and mark, an adult cuts); leave the posts uncut. Slide a
   foam sleeve over each leg piece before any fitting goes on. Fit each
   knee: the elbow or pair at its twist, the coupler on its spigot, glued
   to both pipes; an adult glues outdoors away from the children, and
   the legs rest until day 3. Fit the foot elbow pair, screwed.
3. Day 2: skis, nothing standing. Lay each ski along its wall: cap,
   pipe, tee, pipe, tee, pipe, tee, pipe, tee, pipe, cap, couplers where
   the pipe is in two. Screw every socket. Fill the sacks: two on each
   ski, two at each anchor point 1.5 m beyond each ski end, all against
   the wall. Dry-fit one post stack (Ø 50 coupler, reducer, a foot
   pair), measure from the pipe stop in the coupler to the foot pair's
   centre, and cut each post to put its foot at its height; the posts
   lie along the skis until their legs go on.
4. Day 3: the hub goes up, three adults, the children out of the alley
   until the eighth leg is screwed and every post stayed. Prop the hub
   on the spare Ø 50 length cut to 2.3 m with a cap below and a tee on
   top cradling the spine, standing on a sack under the body, steadied
   by one adult; the ladders are for the two fitting the legs. One leg
   at a time, the long back pairs first, alternating sides: push the
   upper leg into its hip pair and screw it; push the reducer (with its
   coupler) onto the foot pair's spigot; lift the post into the coupler,
   lower it into its tee, turn it to the leg's plan angle, drill and
   screw tee, coupler and reducer; tie that post's two stays at once,
   outward to the anchor sacks beyond its end of the ski and inward to
   the far tee. When both posts of a pair stand, zip-tie two Ø 40
   offcuts across them as rungs, one near the top and one at 1 m. The
   prop comes out after the eighth leg.
5. Day 4: the body. Bin bag over the spine, taped shut; hoops zip-tied
   to the spine; fabric over everything, slits taped at the eight legs;
   eyes on; fangs from two white pipe offcuts, blunt.
6. Dry run two nights before: walk under it, push a post along the wall
   and then toward the alley centre, check every screw, leave it up
   overnight and check again in the morning.

## Safety rules

- Every post is stayed by two cords in the plane of its wall from the
  moment it stands: outward to the anchor sacks beyond its end of the
  ski (shared by the two posts of a pair, 50 kg there) and inward to the
  far tee; the two posts of a pair are joined by two rungs; every ski
  carries two sacks. The page draws the stays and counts the cord.
- Keep every foot at 2.0 m or higher; the page warns below that, and
  the pose it starts from has the lowest feet at exactly 2.0 m. A foot
  at 1.6 m is a handle for a child. A 2.0 m foot keeps children off it,
  but an adult hanging on it buckles a Ø 50 post: nobody hangs on a
  foot, and the adults are told so on the night.
- Knee elbows and the hub glued; two screws in every other socket;
  check all screws every morning, PVC joints work loose in wind.
- Cap every open pipe end. No screw tips outside the pipe. No cord below
  2.3 m across the alley. The stays run along the wall bases and are
  below head height over most of their length, so each carries a strip
  of white tape at eye height and its sacks sit against the wall; no
  cord leaves the wall line by more than 30 cm.
- A push toward the alley centre is the likely one, and only the leg
  acting as a strut (about 100 N) and the ski's ballast friction resist
  it: the post hinges at its tee and the leg bends. Capped ends and the
  foam mean nothing shatters. The dry run tests this push.
- The wall hooks are the first question for the neighbours: four screw
  hooks (one per post pair, at 2 to 2.9 m) with a short cord from each
  pair replace most of the ballast and hold the pull toward the alley
  centre that nothing else does.
- Above a 70 km/h gust forecast, take the body down or the whole spider
  down; a stayed PVC frame has no margin past that. Name who watches the
  forecast (Météo-France vigilance for Charente-Maritime) and who takes
  it down if Daniel is away.
- At night the posts and stays are in a lane people walk: white tape on
  every stay and post base, and a light on the display. The mairie may
  ask for a civil-liability attestation (responsabilité civile).

## Bracing: three ways

Why the cords are there: a PVC post is a pipe standing in a tee. Pushed
along the wall at its top, an unbraced post folds at the tee under
about 15 N, the weight of a bag of sugar; the frame on its own (eight
legs meeting a spine) does not take the wind along the alley either,
since every leg lies across it. Something has to hold each post top
along the wall. The page offers three ways under "Bracing", and the
