The build happens in three places, in this order:

1. Paris, in the flat: the hub is cemented and cured, the fourteen elbow pairs are cemented, the sixteen leg tubes get their fitting sets, and each hip set is trial-fitted to the hub so that every angle is set on the bench.
2. The parcels: three Chronopost boxes, each under 150 cm, collected at the flat and delivered to the neighbours on the island. Daniel carries nothing in the car.
3. The island: the posts and skis (too long for any parcel) are delivered there cut to length. The support structure is built last, since it arrives last, but it is the first thing to stand in the alley. The spider is then put on its posts, dressed, checked, and watched for the week.

Children help on the island during the week: sorting, measuring, marking, making the eye discs. They are out of the alley from the first post up until the spider is on and tested. Two feet are below 2 m, so every post is screwed, never loose.

## How to read this

- Legs are 1R, 1L, 2R, 2L, 3R, 3L, 4R, 4L, always in that order. Pair 1 is the front pair, nearest the street; pair 4 is the back pair. R is the right-hand wall and L the left-hand wall when you stand in the street and look into the alley. R is the garage side (the higher wall, chamfered at the corner); L is the house with the plaque, the climber and the bench. The spider faces the street, so its own left legs stand on the R wall; this manual uses R and L as walls only, never as the spider's sides.
- Positions in the alley: x is the distance along the alley in metres from the centreline of Rue de Trousse Chemise (x = 0, the street mouth of the model), growing into the alley. Height is above the lane. The body centre is at x 6.5 m, 2.603 m up, with the body pitched 45 degrees head up toward the street.
- Labels: the letter before the dash is the wall, the letter after it is the part. 1R-U is the upper tube of leg 1R (hip to knee), 1R-L its lower tube (knee to foot), 1R-P its post, 1R-H its hip set, 1R-K its knee set, 1R-F its foot set, 1R-S its hub stub. 1R-L is a lower tube, not a left anything.
- A set is the one or two PVC elbows that make a joint's angle, plus its coupler when it has one. In a pair, the first elbow is the one nearer the body. The twist is the turn of the second elbow about the first's spigot: twist 0 stacks the two bends in one plane for the largest angle (45 + 20 = 65), twist 180 opposes them for the smallest (45 minus 20 = 25). "45 + 20 at 55" means a 45° elbow then a 20° elbow, the second turned 55 degrees.
- Joint angles: the hip angle is between the stub (straight across the alley) and the upper tube. The hip turn (the page's hip roll) is the turn of the hip's plane about the stub: 0 points the upper horizontally toward the street, 90 straight up. The knee bend is the bend off straight between upper and lower. The knee roll is the turn of the knee's plane about the upper tube from the hip's plane. The foot bend is between the lower tube and the vertical post; the foot roll is the turn of the foot's plane about the lower from the knee's plane. Plan is where the lower points seen from above, for checking only: 0 straight across to the wall, positive toward the street.
- Sign rule for rolls: positive is clockwise when you look from the body end of the tube along it toward the far end (from the hip along the upper toward the knee; from the knee along the lower toward the foot). When you instead look back at a tube's end face toward the body, a positive roll goes anticlockwise round the protractor.
- Fixings: hips, feet, couplers, tees and posts in their tees take two self-drilling screws 4.2 × 16 per socket, a quarter turn apart, 12 mm in from the socket mouth, each through a 3 mm pilot hole drilled through the PVC only (the screw drills the aluminium itself). Knee elbow sockets that hold a tube take two M5 × 60 bolts, crossed at 90 degrees, one 9 mm and one 18 mm from the mouth, through a 5.5 mm hole drilled through socket and tube in one pass, washer and nut outside. The hub and the elbow pairs are PVC cemented. Nothing aluminium is ever cemented.
- Every socket is 27 mm deep. A ring drawn 27 mm from a tube end shows a socket is fully home when its mouth sits on the ring.
- Lengths are in metres unless marked mm; angles in degrees; weights in kg.
- Section headings are the cross-references: "see Seating a leg" means the section with that heading. Each numbered list starts at 1 under its own heading.

### Telling the elbows apart, and which way a pair goes together

- A male/female (M/F) elbow has one plain end and one wide end. The plain end is a spigot: 40 mm outside, the same as the tube, and it slides into another fitting. The wide end is a socket: about 46 mm outside, with a tube-sized hole 27 mm deep. The 20°, 30° and 45° elbows are M/F.
- A female/female (F/F) elbow has two sockets and no spigot. The 67.5° and 87.5° elbows are F/F.
- Quick test: hold a tube end next to each end of the elbow. The end the same size as the tube is the spigot; the wider end is a socket.
- A pair goes together spigot into socket. The first elbow takes the incoming tube, stub or coupler in its socket; its spigot plugs into the second elbow's socket. Every pair in this design starts with a 45° M/F, so no short link of pipe is ever needed between two elbows.
- What follows a set depends on its last elbow. If it is M/F (20, 30 or 45), the set ends in a spigot and a coupler goes on it; the coupler's far socket takes the next tube. If it is F/F (67.5 or 87.5), its second socket takes the next tube directly.
- Elbow dimensions used for the cut lengths (Nicoll Ø 40): socket 27 mm deep, spigot taken as 27 mm; distance from the elbow's centre to the bottom of its socket (Z) 6.5 mm for 20°, 11 for 30°, 13 for 45°, 21 for 67.5°, 31 for 87.5°.

### Marking every piece

## Paris: the hub

The hub is the frame inside the body that carries the eight hips. It and the elbow pairs are the only cemented joints of the spider, and both are made first in Paris because the cement needs 24 hours before it carries load. Finished, the hub is about 0.53 m cap to cap, 0.23 m across the stub tips, 0.05 m thick and about 1 kg. It takes no screws and no bolts. The cement is for PVC on PVC only.

Parts: 4 crosses, 1 PVC pipe Ø 40 × 1 m, 2 caps, the cement. Tools: the ratchet cutter, tape, marker, pencil, a screw to scratch with, file or sandpaper, rag, the table, one aluminium leg tube as a straight edge.

### The numbers

Every socket is taken as 27 mm deep. On a cross the socket bottom is 31 mm from the centre along the spine and 21 mm from the centre on the side branches. A 54 mm spine piece goes 27 mm into each cross, so the crosses touch and sit 116 mm centre to centre. A stub goes 27 mm into its cross and, later, 27 mm into the first socket of its hip set; 36 or 44 mm of it stays visible between the two. The piece table is in The hub's pieces.

Cumulative marks from one end of the pipe, in mm: 98, 196 (the two 98 mm stubs), 286, 376, 466, 556, 646, 736 (the six 90 mm stubs), 790, 844, 898 (the three 54 mm spine pieces), 938, 978 (the two 40 mm ends). If Daniel chooses the 45 + 20 pair for 3L-H, the marks in Decisions apply instead (one 98 mm stub, seven of 90).

### Cut the pipe

1. Push a pencil to the bottom of a cross socket.
2. Mark the pencil at the socket mouth.
3. Measure from the pencil's tip to the mark: expect 27 mm.
4. Whatever it measures, cut the lengths in the table. A deeper socket leaves a few millimetres empty at the bottom; the crosses still touch. A shallower socket sets the crosses apart by twice the difference, which is also fine.
5. Measure the pipe: 1.00 m.
6. Mark the pipe from one end at the cumulative lengths above, all the way round at each mark.
7. Write the name of each piece on it before any cut.
8. An adult cuts at each mark with the ratchet cutter, held square to the pipe, in a few short strokes; cut the long pieces first.
9. File the inside lip of every cut end.
10. Wipe the dust off.
11. Measure every piece against the table; 2 mm either way is fine.

Check: thirteen pieces, each labelled.

### Dry fit, align and mark

1. Push S2 into one of the two in-line (spine) sockets of X1, not a side branch.
2. Push X2 onto it until the two crosses touch.
3. Add S3 and X3.
4. Add S4 and X4.
5. Push S1 into the free spine socket of X1 and S5 into X4.
6. Push a cap onto each end piece until it butts against the cross.
7. Push the six 90 mm stubs into side branches, two per cross, leaving one branch free on X2 and one on X3, on the same side.
8. Lay the hub on the table with the side branches down.
9. Check that every stub lies flat on the table.
10. Turn any cross that rocks on the spine until it lies flat.
11. Sight along the spine from one cap: it must be straight.
12. Lay the leg tube along the top of the four crosses: it must touch all four. The caps are a different thickness and do not count.
13. Stand at one end of the table and look along the spine. If the two free branches on X2 and X3 are on your right, go to the other end and look again: they are now on your left. Do not turn the hub over.
14. The end nearest you is HEAD. Write HEAD on that cap with the marker and scratch HEAD into the cap with the screw tip as well.
15. Write X1, X2, X3, X4 on the crosses from the head back.
16. The face that is up now is the TOP of the hub. Write TOP on X1 and X4 and scratch it in beside the marker. Keep this face up from now on; the hub is never turned over.
17. Name the sides: standing at the HEAD end looking along the spine toward the tail, the stubs on your right are R, the stubs on your left are L (the alley walls seen from the street). The two free branches are on L.
18. Push the two 98 mm stubs into the free L branches of X2 and X3.
19. Write the leg name on each stub and on its branch, and scratch R on X1's R branch and L on its L branch.
20. Draw one straight line along the TOP of the whole hub with the marker, using the leg tube as a ruler, across all four crosses and onto the caps. This is the TOP line. In the alley it faces the body's own up: up and toward the back of the alley, at 45 degrees, because the body is pitched head up toward the street.
21. Draw a short line across each stub onto its branch mouth, so its full depth can be seen when it goes back in.
22. Draw a short line across each spine joint, from cross to cross, in line with the TOP line.
23. Pull everything apart.
24. Keep each piece with its cross.

Check: cap to cap 0.53 m in the dry fit before any cement goes on.

### Cement: how every joint is made

An adult does this with the window open or on the balcony, the children out of the room, no flame nearby. The cement grips in about a minute, so each joint is made in one movement to its marks and then left alone. Eighteen cemented joints (the ten ends of the five spine pieces and the eight stubs) with a 5 minute wait after each cross and after the stubs take about an hour and a half. Do the fourteen elbow pairs (Paris: the legs) in the same session.

1. Wipe both surfaces clean and dry.
2. Brush a thin, even coat over the full depth of the pipe end that goes in.
3. Brush a thinner coat inside the socket.
4. Push home with a quarter turn that ends on the marks, then do not move it.
5. Hold for 30 seconds.
6. Wipe the excess off.
7. Put it down and do not touch that joint for 5 minutes.

### Cement: the order

1. S2 into the back spine socket of X1.
2. X2 onto it, pushed until the crosses touch, the marks in line.
3. Within the first minute, lay the pair branches down on the table and check that all four branch mouths touch the table.
4. Wait 5 minutes.
5. S3 into X2.
6. X3 onto it; check on the table as before; wait 5 minutes.
7. S4 into X3.
8. X4 onto it; check on the table as before; wait 5 minutes.
9. S1 into the free spine socket of X1, S5 into X4.
10. A cap onto each, pushed until it butts against the cross, the HEAD cap at X1.
11. The eight stubs, each into its own named branch, up to its depth mark. Cement only the 27 mm that goes in; the visible part stays clean, because the hip set is screwed onto it, never cemented.
12. Lay the hub branches down on the table.
13. Check within the first minute: all eight stubs touch the table, the TOP line is straight, the leg tube laid along the four crosses touches them all.
14. Leave the hub flat on the table for 24 hours before anything hangs from it.

| Check | Expected |
|---|---|
| Cap to cap | about 0.53 m |
| The run of four crosses, touching | 0.46 m |

A hub that rocks by a millimetre or two is fine: the hip sets turn on the stubs and take that up. A visible bend in the spine is not fine; it puts the two sides' hips out of plane and the whole spider would lean. The cement does not come apart, so a bent spine means another metre of pipe and four more crosses. The dry fit and the marks exist to avoid that.

### Marking a tube

Every roll is set by two lines drawn along the tube. Do this on both tubes of every leg before the sleeve goes on.

1. Lay the tube on a flat table.
2. Wedge it with two books so it cannot roll.
3. Lay the marker flat on the table with its tip against the tube and slide it the full length. This straight line is line 0.
4. On the upper, line 0 is where the hip set leans: the side of the tube the hip set's open socket points to. On the lower, line 0 is the side that faces the upper tube at the knee.
5. Cut a 2 mm notch into the tube's rim on line 0 at both ends with the corner of the file, so the line can be found again when the marker has rubbed off.
6. Go to the far end of the tube (the knee end of an upper, the foot end of a lower) and look at the end face.
7. Centre the protractor on the face and put its 0 on line 0.
8. Read the angle from the table below. You are looking back toward the hub, so a positive roll goes anticlockwise round the protractor and a negative one clockwise. Mark the rim.
9. Draw a line 100 mm long from that mark along the tube: line K on an upper, line F on a lower.
10. Check with a paper strip wrapped round the tube: the distance along the strip between line 0 and the new line is 0.35 mm per degree, as in the table.
11. Check the label, the arrow toward the body and the 27 mm ring at both ends.

### Cementing the elbow pairs

Fourteen pairs (listed under The fitting sets). Do them all in one session with the hub, by an adult, with the window open, the day before the legs are built. Marks on the elbows: marker, not pencil. For the angle check you need two of the aluminium tubes, a coupler, masking tape and a clear patch of floor.

1. Take the two elbows of one pair; the first is the one named first (the 45 in 45 + 20 and 45 + 67.5, either 45 in 45 + 45).
2. On each elbow draw a line along the outside of its bend, from end to end, onto the spigot of the first and onto the socket of the second.
3. Push the second onto the first's spigot dry, with the two lines in line. Both bends now go the same way; this is twist 0.
4. Pull it off.
5. Centre the protractor on the end of the first elbow's spigot, with 0 on its line, and mark the twist from the table on the spigot. Either direction gives the same angle; the two choices are mirror images and both are right.
6. Push the second on dry with its line on the mark.
7. Push a tube into the first elbow's socket, and a tube into the set's other end (straight into a socket, or through a coupler pushed onto a spigot).
8. Lay the whole thing on the floor on two strips of masking tape, one under each tube.
9. Draw along each tube on its tape, then lift the set away.
10. Extend the first tube's line past the crossing and measure, with the protractor, how far the second tube's line turns off it: within 2 degrees of the joint angle in the table (58 for 1R-H, 93 for 1R-F, and so on). A wrong elbow shows here; a small twist error hardly moves the angle.
11. Draw one short line across the joint, over both elbows, so the position can be found again.
12. Pull the elbows apart and take the tubes out.
13. Brush cement on the outside of the spigot and the inside of the socket.
14. Push home with a quarter turn that ends with the two short lines meeting, within ten seconds, and hold for thirty seconds. Then do not move it.
15. Wipe the surplus, write the set's label (1R-H) on it, and set it aside for 24 hours.
16. When the set is dry, sight into the end that will go on its tube: for a knee or foot set the first elbow's socket, for a hip set the spigot end (where the coupler goes). The far end sits off to one side. Mark that side on the rim with an arrow. This is the set's lean mark; it goes on the tube's line.

Check: fourteen sets, each labelled, each with a lean mark, none turned after cementing.

### Setting the hips on the hub

Every hip set is trial-fitted to its stub on the hub, screwed at its turn, then unscrewed again, so that on the island the set only slides back onto its stub and the same screws go into the same holes. Nothing is measured on the ladder. Do this the day the legs are done, with the hub cured 24 hours.

The hip turn on the page is measured with the spider standing in the alley: 0 horizontal toward the street, 90 plumb up. On the floor the spine lies level, so you set the page's turn less 45 degrees (the body's pitch): seen along the stub from outside the hub, the turn from the spine toward the HEAD cap, turning toward the TOP line; past 90 the upper lies toward the tail cap. Every upper turns toward TOP, so with the hub lying TOP up all eight rise off the floor and the hub is never turned over.

A protractor reading is hard on a tube over a metre long, so the turn is set by where the upper's far end lands, measured to the knee's centre: the centre of the knee set's first elbow when the set is on, or 4 to 5 cm past the tube's end for 1R. Along is measured on the floor from the middle of the cross, parallel to the spine, toward the HEAD cap or the tail cap as stated. Up is the height above the spine's axis: measure from the floor to the middle of the tube and take off the spine axis's height. Out is from the spine's line to the point under the knee. 3 cm either way is within about 2 degrees.

### If a foot set does not meet its post top

Measure first: by how much, and in which direction, the foot set's socket misses the post top. Then pick the smallest fix from this list. The first ones are harmless; the last ones change the pose and are done once. The hub itself can be moved only before the back pair is fixed. The single-elbow errors listed in The fitting sets (up to 2.5 degrees at 3L's hip and 2R's foot) are the usual cause of a miss of a few centimetres.

- The socket is up to 1 cm above the post top: the post is not fully down in its tee, or the leg is not fully home at the knee or hip. Push each socket down to its ring. If all three are home, lift the post out of its tee, drop a 40 mm disc of wood or plastic no thicker than 1 cm into the tee's branch, and put the post back; the post then still has 17 mm in the tee. Never more than 1 cm: a thicker shim leaves too little post in the tee, and the error is elsewhere.
- The socket is up to 3 cm below the post top: shorten the post. Unscrew the post from its tee if it is screwed. Lift it out. Measure the cut twice. Cut the amount off the top end with the hacksaw, square, in steps of no more than 1 cm. File the burr. Redraw the ring. Put the post back with the old screw holes lined up and the same screws.
- The socket is more than 3 cm off in height, or off along the wall by more than a few centimetres, and the back pair is not yet fixed: the hub is in the wrong place. Move the hub on the prop until the knee of this leg is at the table's position, then try again. Check the other seated leg again after moving.
- The socket is off along the wall by a few centimetres: turn the lower a little in the knee set, or the knee set a little on its upper if that is where the error is (the knee roll). A degree at the knee moves the foot about 2 cm for the long legs (1R, 1L, 3R, 4R) and 1 to 1.5 cm for the others. The foot set's socket must still point straight down; if it then leans, the hub or the hip turn is off, not the knee.
- The socket is off along the wall and this is the first leg seated on that wall: slide the whole ski a few centimetres along the wall. It moves all four tees of that side together, so do it only before the second leg of that side is seated, and re-pack the ski's gaps afterwards.
- The socket is off across the alley (toward the wall or toward the middle): never lean the post more than 1 cm per metre of its height to meet it, and never move a ski across. Move the hub instead if the back pair is not yet fixed; otherwise take this leg's lower out, re-measure its knee, and look for the error there. A hip turn off by 10 degrees moves a knee 6 to 20 cm, most on the long front legs and the back pair.
- A hip turn is wrong: take the star down to the ground to change it (two new screw holes a quarter turn from the old ones, the old holes taped over). Never unscrew a hip with the hub on the prop.
- Insertion less than 15 mm at any socket is not a fix. A post cut too short is not fixable: cut only after measuring twice.
- A tube that has to be bent or pulled to meet is wrong somewhere else: stop and measure the knee again.

### Fixing a leg

Once it is seated. Safety glasses on for every hole: the knee holes are above your head.

1. Knee, 1L, 2L, 4R: drill a 5.5 mm hole through the 67.5's socket and the lower inside it, 9 mm from the socket mouth, straight through both walls; push an M5 bolt through with a washer under its head; washer and nut on the far side, tightened with the spanner, the head held with the screwdriver; drill the second hole a quarter turn round, 18 mm from the mouth, and fit the second bolt.
2. Knee, 1R, 2R, 3R, 3L, 4L: drill a 3 mm pilot through the knee coupler's socket 12 mm from its mouth and drive a screw; the second a quarter turn round, 12 mm from the mouth (or two crossed bolts as in the step above, if Daniel chose bolts there).
3. Foot: drill a 3 mm pilot through the foot socket (or coupler) 12 mm from its mouth and drive a screw; the second a quarter turn round.
4. Tee: drill a 3 mm pilot through the tee's branch 12 mm from its mouth, on the face that looks along the alley, and drive a screw; the second a quarter turn round, on the face toward the alley centre.
5. Pull the leg gently at the knee: nothing turns, nothing slides.

### The fabric

The fabric is 145 cm wide. The widest hoops are 1.26 m round, so the width goes round the body with about 19 cm of overlap underneath. Length 1 (1.2 m) covers the abdomen and the tail, from hoop 2 back; length 2 (0.8 m) the head, from hoop 2 forward. Legs 3 and 4 pass through length 1, legs 1 and 2 through length 2.

1. Length 1: lay it over the top of the body from hoop 2 to the tail, equal amounts hanging each side, stretched just enough to lie smooth.
2. On each side, cut from the lower edge straight up to where a pair 3 or pair 4 leg meets the body. Stop the cut at the hip set.
3. Bring the fabric down past the legs, close each cut round the hip set or its coupler, whichever the fabric reaches, and tape all the way round so no edge flaps. If grey PVC shows at a hip, wrap it first in black tape.
4. Tape the cut's edges together below the leg, then bring the two lower edges together underneath with the 19 cm overlap.
5. Zip-tie the fabric to hoop 2 at four points round the hoop (pierce the fabric and go round the noodle).
6. Gather the fabric past hoop 5 at the tail, twist it and close it with two zip ties. Trim the surplus to 10 cm below the ties and tape the end. This is the tie at the back.
7. Length 2: lay it over the head from hoop 2 forward, overlapping length 1 by 15 cm at hoop 2. Cut up from the lower edges for the pair 1 and pair 2 legs and close the cuts as in steps 3 and 4.
8. Zip-tie length 2 to hoop 2 at four points through both layers.
9. Pull it forward over hoop 1 and the HEAD cap, gather it under the chin just behind hoop 1, two zip ties, trim, tape. The fabric over hoop 1 is the face.

### The eyes

Make the discs on the first island build day so the silicone has cured before the eyes go up. Neutral silicone skins in about 30 minutes; give a bead at least 24 hours, 48 to be sure.

1. Cut eight plastic discs: two of about 16 cm for the 13 cm eyes, six of about 9 cm for the small eyes. Round the edges with the file.
2. Drill two 5.5 mm holes in each disc near the edge, 2 cm apart, at what will be the disc's top.
3. Clean the back of each eye (peel its adhesive backing if it has one). Run a bead of silicone round the back of the eye 1 cm in from its edge, press it on the disc, and run a second bead round the outside of the eye where it meets the disc.
4. Leave the discs flat for 24 hours, out of the children's reach.
5. If the small-eye pack has more than six eyes, make the extras into spare discs now and keep them for the week after. The kits as listed have no spares.
6. Up the ladder, with the fabric on. The face is the 26 cm circle of fabric over hoop 1. The spider is pitched 45 degrees up, so the whole pattern sits low on the face, its centre about a third of the way up from the chin, and every eye is tilted to look toward the street, not at the sky.
7. The two big eyes: side by side, centres 15 cm apart, just below the pattern's centre. With 13 cm eyes that leaves a 2 cm gap between them.
8. Pass a zip tie through each hole of a disc, through the fabric and round the lower part of hoop 1 behind it. Pull tight. Push a 5 cm piece of noodle offcut between the top of the disc and the hoop to set the tilt, and tape it.
9. The six small eyes, two zip ties each, same tilt: one beside and a little below each big eye, out near the rim (the two about 22 cm apart); two close together above the centre, touching; and one above and outside each big eye near the rim (about 22 cm apart).
10. Trim every tie tail and turn the tie heads to the back.
11. Fangs, if there are offcuts for them: two white PVC pieces about 10 cm, ends filed round, each tied with two zip ties through the fabric to the bottom of hoop 1 under the big eyes, 10 cm apart, pointing down and a little forward. A socket with one screw is not done.
2. Every knee is above 2.5 m. The parts a child reaches are the foot sets, the lower tube ends and the posts of 4R (foot 1.49 m) and 4L (foot 1.72 m), and the bottom of every post in its tee: every screw head there sits flush, no screw tip stands out of a tube, and no bolt end is bare anywhere a hand reaches.
3. Every post is plumb. Hold the level, or the phone's level app, on the post at chest height on two faces 90 degrees apart. Correct a lean by packing under the ski with noodle or wood offcuts, never by forcing the post in its tee.
4. Both skis lie flat on the lane, inserts in, 2.83 m apart centre to centre (3.09 m from street end to street end). Ski R runs from x 4.322 to 7.590 and stands about 1 cm off the wall at its street end and up to 3.6 cm at its back end; ski L runs from 5.570 to 8.180 and stands about 5 cm off at its street end and 2.5 cm at its back end. Every gap is packed with foam or wood so the ski cannot move toward the wall.
5. Push each post along the wall, then toward the centre of the alley, with one hand, firmly. Nothing turns in a tee, nothing creaks, the ski does not slide.
6. Clearance: the lowest point over the walkway in the design is 1.99 m, which is the lower tube of leg 4R where it is 30 cm out from the R wall; the body's underside is 2.18 m. Walk the lane along the centre and along each side with a hand raised, and measure under 4R and 4L with the tape. Nothing further than 30 cm from a wall is below 1.99 m. Nearer the R wall than that, 4R's tube comes down to its foot at 1.49 m.
7. Nothing a child can pull off: tug every eye, every hoop, the fangs, the tail tie, each taped cut, and the foot sets of 4R and 4L sideways; the posts do not move.
8. Every open tube end is capped: the two spine caps, the four ski ends. No raw aluminium edge is exposed anywhere a hand reaches.
9. No cord anywhere. The design has no stays and no ballast; if anyone has tied a rope to a post or a ski, take it off. Each side frame stands on its posts in their tees, and the legs brace against the far wall.
10. Seen at night: the alley is lit, so no tape on the posts (Daniel, 15 October); a light on the display only if the eyes are lit (the lit eyes if chosen, otherwise a battery lamp from the house tied to hoop 4 under the fabric or to the backbone at the tail). Walk through once in the dark with a torch held at waist height: the eight bands show, and the lowest legs show.
11. Check the forecast. Above a forecast of 70 km/h gusts (rafales) for Charente-Maritime on Météo-France, take the body down, or the whole spider down. Name tonight who watches the forecast and who takes it down if Daniel is away.
12. Tell every adult on the night: nobody hangs on a leg or a post. A post is a tube in a tee, not a climbing frame.
13. The lane is closed to vehicles while the spider is up: the sign is at the alley mouth, the neighbours who drive in know, and the bins go out to the street on collection mornings. The mairie's request has been sent and its answer is on the phone.

Rules from the plan that still apply:

- Two fixings in every socket; check them every morning the spider is up. PVC joints work loose in wind.
- Feet 4R (1.49 m) and 4L (1.72 m) are within a child's reach. Their posts must be screwed in their tees, never loose, and the children are told that the back legs are not for hanging on.
- Cap every open end. No screw tips outside a tube.
- No reflective tape: the alley is lit.
- The mairie may ask for a civil-liability attestation (responsabilité civile); keep it on the phone.

### The week after

One look every day, and always after wind or rain. Five minutes, from the ground and the ladder.

1. Screws: look at each foot and each post tee. A screw that has backed out shows a bright shank. Drive it home. If it spins without biting, take it out, drill a new 3 mm pilot hole a quarter turn round the socket, 12 mm from the socket mouth, and drive a new screw there. Leave the old hole.
2. Knee bolts: put the 8 mm spanner on the nut and a screwdriver in the bolt head. Tighten until the washer bites the PVC; do not crush the socket.
3. Posts: the level on two faces again. Re-pack the ski's gaps if a post has leaned.
4. Fabric: a cut that has opened gets fresh gaffer tape; a flapping edge gets a zip tie to the nearest hoop. Water held in a fold is pushed out toward the tail.
5. Eyes: a disc that has turned is straightened and its ties tightened. A lost eye: cut the two ties, tie on a spare disc if one was made, same two holes, same tilt. Otherwise stick a new eye on a new disc with silicone and wait 24 hours before it goes up; do not put uncured silicone on the hoop.
6. After rain, feel the lane under each ski. Wet stone with algae is slippery; if a ski has moved, measure 3.09 m between the street ends and 2.83 m between the skis, push it back to that, and re-pack its gaps.
7. Forecast every evening. 70 km/h gusts: the body comes down (fabric, eyes and hoops are off in about 20 minutes); worse, the legs come off and the posts come out.
8. The sign at the mouth is still up and readable; the bins went out to the street on the collection morning.

### Takedown

The reverse of the build. Before anything is unscrewed, mark every joint so it goes back the same next year.

1. With a marker, draw one line across each socket and its tube at every hip, knee, foot and post tee, and scratch it in. Next year the lines meet again and the angles are right without the protractor.
2. Eyes off: cut the ties, keep the discs in a box with any spares.
3. Fabric off: cut the tail and chin ties, cut the ties at hoop 2, peel the tape at the legs, pull the two lengths off and fold them.
4. Bin bag liner off and binned. Hoops: cut their ties; hoops 1, 4 and 5 come off whole over the ends, hoops 2 and 3 are opened at their joins. Backbone off.
5. Prop the hub before the first leg comes off, as it was propped going up. Then one leg at a time, alternating sides, in the reverse of the integration order (the last leg on comes off first). One person holds the post; the other, up the ladder, takes the two screws out of the hip set's first socket and pulls the set off its stub (the hip set and its coupler stay on the upper tube). Then take the two screws out of the post's tee, lift the post out of the tee and walk the leg down with its post to the lane.
6. On the ground, take the foot set off the post (two screws) and keep it with the lower tube.
7. Hub down: spine, crosses, stubs and caps stay cemented as one piece.
8. Split a leg at the knee only if it will not store whole; otherwise leave the knee fixed. Keep each leg's upper, lower and post together with its label (1R to 4L).
9. Tees unscrewed from the skis, ski pieces apart, inserts out. Put every screw back into the hole it came from, two turns in, so it is not lost. Bag each leg's loose fittings, labelled.
10. Store the structure as pieces: eight posts and ten ski pieces bundled and labelled by side and order from the street end, the eight tees and four inserts in one bag.
11. Sweep the lane where the skis lay. Return the bench. Take the sign down and tell the neighbours the lane is open.

What goes where: the hub, legs and fittings can stay on the island in the house for next year; nothing needs to go back to Paris unless Daniel wants it there.
